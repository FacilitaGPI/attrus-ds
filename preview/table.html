Prefer: a table when rows are comparable along shared columns. One hierarchy mechanism per table — a subrow when the child shares the parent's columns, a detail panel when it has its own shape and stays small, a drawer once it earns navigation of its own.

01Intent

A table exists so rows can be compared along shared columns. If the reader will never scan one column across rows, the data is a list or a set of cards — not a table.

Reach for it when

decision
SituationUseWhy
Rows share attributes the reader comparesDataTableColumn alignment is the whole point
One record, many attributesDescription listNothing to compare across; a grid wastes the alignment
Items differ in shapeCardsForcing them into columns invents empty cells
Under ~4 rows, 2 columnsKey–value pairsA header row costs more than it explains

Row hierarchy — the ladder

02When to use

Tables are for comparing many records across the same set of attributes. If users scan down a single column to compare values, a table is right. If each record needs rich, varied layout, use Cards instead.

Do / don't

Six rules that decide most table questions before they reach a design review.

guidance

Right-align all numbersUse .dt-num so decimals line up. Tabular figures are on by default at the .dt level.

Don't center-align numeric columnsCentered figures break vertical scanning and make magnitude impossible to compare.

Keep one primary action per rowA trailing .dt-actions cluster or a clickable row — not a wall of buttons in every cell.

Don't exceed ~7 columns on desktopPast that, move secondary attributes into a .dt-cell-stack sub-line or a detail drawer.

Always show count + paginationUsers need to know the total and where they are. Pair .count .total with .dt-pagination .range.

Don't tint rows per categoryRow colour means state (selected / error / muted) only. Use a status pill or lead tone for category.

Progress — tone × shape
Shape and tone are independent. Shape answers is the count meaningful? — a bar at 60% invites estimation, a filled third segment states “3 of 5”. Tone answers what does this progress mean?, using the same semantic pairs as the pills.
default Neutral progress — a batch, an upload. Keeps the brand gradient, which reads richer than a flat fill.
success Complete, or on track. At 100% the tone is what tells the reader it finished rather than stalled.
warning Stalled or needs attention. The tone says it without a second column of copy.
danger A failed run. States a fact — it does not imply anything is still happening.
critical Danger that is also urgent: a cut-off hours away, a limit about to bind. The pulse carries the time pressure colour cannot, and it stops under prefers-reduced-motion.
info Informational progress with no judgement attached — a sync, an index rebuild.

13Subrows — an item glued to an item

For an entry that belongs to another and shares its columns: the fee under its payment, the reversal under its charge, the leg under its transfer. This is row hierarchy, not a detail panel — the child fills the parent's columns, so the pair reads as one block. The parent gives up its bottom border (that is what glues them), the child takes a sunken background and one type step down, and the last child of the group restores the rule.

Why the double wrapper — A <tr> cannot be animated and display:grid on a <td> destroys column alignment, so the transition lives on .dt-subrow-inner inside each cell — a grid going grid-template-rows: 0fr → 1fr. That needs no magic pixel height, so a subrow that wraps to two lines on mobile still animates correctly. Cell padding moves to .dt-subrow-cell for the same reason — and it starts at zero, arriving with the reveal: a grid item's padding counts toward its automatic minimum size, so leaving 8px on it floors the 0fr track at 16px and a closed group still shows a gap. min-height: 0 does not remove that floor; transitioning the padding does. Measured: 1px closed (the hairline alone) against 39px open.

When the child is a different kind of thing, its columns cannot be the parent's — an audit trail has timestamp, actor and action, not amount and method.
